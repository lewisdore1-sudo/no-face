 The Shifting Mansion: How to Program the Room SwappingTo build the Blue Prince room-shifting style efficiently, you should use a method called Level Streaming / Sub-level Swapping. Instead of creating one giant map, you will divide your mansion into chunks.1. The Dynamic "Room Loader" MethodKeep your main mansion frame (like the central grand hallway, staircases, and the exit doors leading to the botanical garden) permanently loaded in your Persistent Level.Build your variant rooms separately (e.g., Room_A_Variant_1 is a tidy bedroom, Room_A_Variant_2 has furniture flipped upside down, Room_A_Variant_3 has blood on the walls).Place an invisible Trigger Box inside the doorway or hallway just outside the room.Using your Level Blueprint, you script it so that the moment the player steps out of the room and crosses the trigger box, the game unloads the current variant and loads a completely different variant in the exact same physical coordinates. When they turn around and walk back in, the room has seamlessly changed.2. The Prop Shifting Method (Actor Blueprints)If you don't want to swap the whole room structure, you can swap the items inside it:Make an Actor Blueprint called BP_MansionRoom.Inside this Blueprint, create a few different lists (Arrays) of props (e.g., Array 1: normal chairs and tables; Array 2: broken chairs, clocks frozen at 3:00 AM, creepy paintings).Use a Random Integer in Range node triggered by the player exiting the room to hide the old props and spawn/unhide the new ones.🌿 The Outdoor Botanical Garden: The Contrast of HorrorThe botanical garden serves as a brilliant structural anchor. In Chilla's Art games, moving between a cramped, dim interior and an open outdoor space creates amazing tension.The Illusion of Safety: Make the botanical garden feel like the player's "safe haven" at first. Give it open skies, plants, and natural moonlight.The Dread Set-in: As the game progresses and the mansion inside becomes more twisted, start altering the garden. Make the plants look withered when the player returns outside. Use Unreal’s Exponential Height Fog to slowly roll thick, low-lying fog across the garden paths, drastically cutting down the player's visibility.Fluorescent vs. Natural Light: Inside the mansion, use harsh, buzzing, sickly fluorescent lighting. Outside in the garden, use crisp, sharp moonlight casting long, dark shadows through the greenhouse glass and tree branches.👣 The Player Flow (The Chilla's Art Loop)To give it that classic indie horror flavor, design it around mundane puzzle tasks.The player is given a simple objective: "Find 3 missing keys in the east wing" or "Water the rare orchids in the botanical garden."They leave the grand hall to go do the task.While they are busy doing the task, your triggers swap the grand hall behind them.When they attempt to walk back to safety, the environment is unrecognizable, blocking their original path and forcing them deeper into the mansion where your hand-drawn entities await.This setup sounds incredibly atmospheric!
